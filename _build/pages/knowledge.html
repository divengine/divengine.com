Requirements, decisions, models & knowledge

Structured knowledge, not a pile of documents

In Studio, intent, rules, requirements, decisions, models and documentation are separate records with explicit relationships. Each one can be reviewed, versioned and found on its own, and each one points to what it depends on.

Business first, then the system

From business rules to system requirements

Describe the business (actors, workers, business use cases and rules) before the software. Then capture system actors, use cases and functional and non-functional requirements, each with a state, a priority, a risk and an owner.

  • Lifecycles with explicit transitions: draft, in review, approved, baselined
  • Revisions of every change, with who and why
  • Use cases that include or specialize others, never in a cycle

Reasoning as records

Decisions, risks and evidence have a place

The parts of engineering that usually stay implicit get their own record types, with codes and revisions like any requirement, and typed traces to the records they explain.

{{icon:flow}}

Decisions DEC

An architectural or design decision with its rationale and the alternatives considered, linked to the requirements and models it shapes.

{{icon:shield}}

Risks RSK

Identified risks with their impact assessment and mitigation strategy, and traces to the records that mitigate them.

{{icon:check}}

Evidence EVD

Evidence of engineering activity, review, verification or validation, which other records can point to.

{{icon:book}}

Standards STD

References to the standards and regulations a solution has to respect, traceable like anything else.

{{icon:layers}}

Knowledge references KR

External knowledge a team relies on, recorded and linked rather than remembered.

{{icon:milestone}}

Method fragments MF

Reusable practices, techniques or process fragments a team adopts, kept as records that work can refer to.

Diagram modelers Available

Models next to the records they describe

Studio has a diagram platform: diagrams are records with revisions and moderation, notations are plugins, previews are sanitized SVG, and diagram elements can link to the records they represent.

BPMN

A BPMN 2.0 modeler (bpmn-js) runs inside Studio, with the engine bundled, not loaded from a CDN. Elements link to the actors, rules and use cases they represent, and those records list the diagrams that reference them.

  • Safe SVG previews with clickable links
  • Embed any diagram in an article or a description
{{shot:bpmn|A BPMN diagram of claim intake in Divengine Studio}}

UML

Write PlantUML in a split editor and see the diagram live: use cases, classes, sequences, states, components. The engine runs in the browser, external includes are rejected, and bound elements link to real records.

{{shot:uml|A UML use case diagram written in PlantUML in a split editor}}
Modeling Foundation Planned

From diagrams to semantic models

Today a diagram is a document whose elements can link to records. The planned Modeling Foundation adds a semantic model repository, where model elements and their relationships are themselves versioned, access-controlled records, and diagrams become views of them. Existing diagram types keep working unchanged.

Planned

Semantic model repository

Development-scoped models, elements and typed relationships with stable identity, revisions, validation and traces to other records.

Planned

Language extensions

Metamodel extensions for UML, BPMN, IFML, ArchiMate, DMN and CMMN, each keeping its own semantics.

Planned

Concept maps & UI modeling

Concept maps as views of curated concepts and propositions, and a UI modeler for interface and form design.

These are roadmap items, designed in the open in the Studio issue tracker. See the roadmap for what is planned and in what order.

Knowledge base

Documentation that knows what it talks about

Write articles in a tree per Development: overviews, architecture, how-tos, onboarding. Mention [[REQ-0001]] or [[UC-0002]] and the text links to the record, which lists the article under “Mentioned in”. The narrative stays readable; the records stay authoritative.

  • Hierarchy of articles with sections and sub-articles
  • Diagrams embedded in the text, always the current revision
  • Access follows the Development
